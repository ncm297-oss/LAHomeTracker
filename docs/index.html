How the math works

  1. Both of you start with the same cash: price + buy closing costs. The owner puts it into the house; the renter invests it at the expected return.
  2. Each year the owner pays property tax (on the Prop 13 assessed value, growing at most the cap and never above market), insurance, maintenance and HOA. The renter pays rent. Whoever pays less invests the difference into the renter's portfolio (a negative contribution means the owner was cheaper that year).
  3. Portfolio tax: taxed at sale compounds pre-tax and pays the combined long-term rate (federal LTCG + NIIT + CA) on the gain at the end; taxed yearly reduces the return by the federal marginal rate each year (Treasury interest is CA-exempt); pre-tax ignores tax.
  4. At sale the owner nets value × (1 − sell costs) − tax, where tax applies to the gain over cost basis beyond the $500K exclusion.
  5. Wealth difference = owner net − renter net. Breakeven price, appreciation and rent are found by solving for the input that makes the difference zero.

Not modelled yet: mortgages, the SALT / mortgage-interest deduction, realising gains on portfolio withdrawals. Financing is Phase 1B.
